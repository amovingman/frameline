# Owner performance acceptance

Frameline’s supported owner workflow streams media hashes and transfers, keeps scans outside the Electron main thread, exposes cancellation, and resumes verified transfer chunks after an explicit request. The current acceptance run uses the retained editor-backed **50-picture-clip /50-reference export** and the established **16 MiB +31-byte synthetic media size**. It creates no Resolve project, imports no larger timeline, contacts no public host, and sends no telemetry.

Run `node scripts/verify-personal-performance.cjs` to collect a new report. It needs permission to bind disposable numeric-loopback HTTPS listeners. The command creates a private temporary directory, generates deterministic synthetic bytes in1MiB blocks, starts three fresh production TLS service processes and three fresh transfer-client processes, verifies every owned process has exited, and removes its disposable files. Test certificate trust applies only inside each transfer client; certificate verification remains enabled. Credentials, signed capabilities, private paths and endpoint addresses are absent from the saved report.

The report is `phase-0/personal-performance-proof.json`. `node scripts/audit-personal-performance.cjs` checks it without starting listeners, transferring bytes, accessing Resolve, changing journals, or rewriting evidence. Source digests and the independently verified retained corpus bind the proof to the implementation it measured. Rerun the verifier after changes to pinned sources.

## What the check measures

- Three independent cold worker scans and their hash-bound cache rescans, each checked against the exact export SHA,50 clips,50 references and2400 timeline frames. Cold includes worker startup; cached still rereads/hashes the XML and refreshes media availability. The old exported media locations remain missing; these scan timings do not imply media readiness.
- Three streamed production file inspections with exact expected SHA and byte count. Progress increments never exceed1MiB, matching the production bounded read path. The fixture generator and independent final hash also stream; no full-media buffer is needed by the harness.
- Explicit scan cancellation during its reading phase, followed by a verified50-clip scan; and hash cancellation after the first1MiB, with no further reported bytes. Request-to-settled durations describe these chosen cancellation points. They do not claim cancellation of every OS read or remote request.
- A cancelled upload retaining exactly chunk0; a new process and restarted service explicitly upload only chunks1–16, including the31-byte tail. A cancelled download leaves its final destination absent and retains a verified private chunk0. Another fresh process and restarted service explicitly fetch only chunks1–16 and publish the exact final SHA with mode0600.
- Elapsed monotonic wall time, process CPU, before/after and sampled RSS, process-lifetime maximum RSS, and5ms heartbeat observations. The heartbeat measures this proof client’s event loop; the existing real-Electron large-project evidence separately covers renderer/main responsiveness.

All work in the fresh-process harness is explicitly requested by the verifier. It does not demonstrate a new desktop automatic-resume behavior; the established desktop transfer proof independently verifies that restarts remain paused until explicit resume. Upload identity hashes deliberately restart from byte0 on every attempt and again before publication. Resuming skips acknowledged network chunks, **not** the identity checks. Plain SHA-256 state is never serialized or trusted across restart. Media-audit jobs similarly restart hashing from the beginning when resumed; that remains explicit in their retained evidence.

## Acceptance and limits

This is a bounded smoke acceptance on the owner machine. Each50-clip scan must finish within5seconds, each measured operation within30seconds, and the chosen cancellation points within1second. Exact hashes/counts, bounded reported hash increments, cold worker heartbeats, correct chunk reuse, an absent partial destination, terminal prior processes, and source bytes unchanged are required. These generous guards detect stalls or broken workflows; they are not product latency promises. The three-run p50/p95 use nearest rank; with three samples p95 is the observed maximum, not a statistically representative tail estimate.

RSS is observed rather than proven as a hard memory ceiling. A short operation can finish between5ms samples. `sampledPeakRss` covers timer samples plus the initial observation; `rssAfter` may exceed that sampled value. `processMaxRssKiB` is the OS-reported lifetime high-water mark, which can include earlier work in that client. Bounded production chunk reads and progress increments establish the implemented streaming path; they do not prove constant total process RSS.

The retained prior large-project proof covers disposable native50/500/10000-clip exports, worker cancellation during parse/media checks, bounded DOM windows and navigation. The prior media-audit proof covers an8GiB sparse hashing fixture, explicit cancellation and restart, and says that hashing rereads from the beginning. The prior16MiB desktop TLS proof verifies native picker/consent, paused restart and exact upload/download bytes. This new report pins those observations as historical context; it does not relabel them as fresh native measurements.

Customer projects, varied working media, WAN throughput, public hosting, Studio, new editor versions, native SDK write/reopen timing, power loss and concurrent unrelated writers remain outside this owner performance claim. The separately audited native operation measurements retain their actual coarse-clock limits. No native write or Apply authority follows from a passing performance check.
